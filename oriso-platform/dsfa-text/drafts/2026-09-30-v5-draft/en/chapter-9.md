Processing aims to enable accessible, confidential counselling independent of location for people seeking advice. [Controller’s name] thereby fulfils its [statutory / legal / church] mandate to support people in situations of distress and conflict; the basis is [reference to mission statement, statutes or legal mandate]. The purpose is legitimate because it reduces barriers to access: it particularly reaches people who would not visit a counselling centre because of shame, mobility constraints, time constraints or fear of stigmatisation. Processing additionally aims to offer digital counselling with the same quality and confidentiality as in-person counselling.

Processing is suitable for achieving the purpose. A central platform discoverable under an established name and usable under a pseudonym demonstrably reaches people who would otherwise not seek help; this is supported by [metrics, e.g. number of first enquiries, proportion of anonymous first contacts, survey/evaluation dated [date]]. The technical procedures used are suitable for actually providing the promised confidentiality: counselling content is transmitted and stored with end-to-end encryption, so neither the operator nor its service providers can access it. The unified platform also enables common standards of quality, security and continuing education that individual organisations could not achieve separately.

No less intrusive, equally suitable means is available. The following were assessed and rejected: (1) dispensing with all registration — this would make continuous counselling across multiple contacts and development of a trusting relationship impossible; registration-free live chat remains available for initial contact as a less intrusive option; (2) decentralised individual organisation solutions — these fragment the service, exceed smaller organisations’ technical and financial capabilities, and substantially reduce discoverability for advice seekers; (3) [other assessed alternative, e.g. telephone-only or in-person counselling] — [reason for rejection]. The scope of data collected is limited to what is necessary: only the postcode is mandatory to assign the responsible counselling centre; the username is system-generated and all other information is voluntary.

The balancing of interests favours processing. The sensitivity of counselling content and the particular vulnerability of some advice seekers weigh in favour of data subjects; the service’s benefit and depth of protective measures weigh in favour of processing. Four aspects are decisive: (1) **voluntary participation** — use, registration and all additional information are voluntary, and withdrawal is effective immediately at any time; (2) **data minimisation** — the username is system-generated, only the postcode is mandatory, and there is no tracking, profiling or advertising; (3) **limited processing activities** — the operator does not evaluate counselling data; statistics are aggregated and subject to small-cell suppression; (4) **extensive technical and organisational measures** — particularly continuous end-to-end encryption of all counselling formats, self-deletion of messages and accounts, mandatory two-factor authentication for counsellors and the logged handover procedure. Remaining risks and their associated measures are detailed in the risk analysis (Annex 1); issues not yet finally addressed are [list of open issues taken from Chapter 10].
