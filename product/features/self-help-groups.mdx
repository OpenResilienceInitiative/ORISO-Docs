A **self-help group** (*Gesprächskreis*) is a scheduled group conversation that a counsellor runs
for many clients at once: a topic, a date, a start time, a duration, optional repeats, and a
format (text, audio or video). Clients reach it through an **invite link** that the counsellor
shares.

This page describes **what happens when someone opens that link**. It is written for counsellors,
agency administrators and data protection officers. The technical notes for developers are at the
end.

<Info>
Status (23.09.2026): the rules on this page are decided. Parts of them are still in review and not
on any server yet — see [4.10.7 Delivery status](#4107-delivery-status).
</Info>

## 4.10.1 The invite link

The counsellor copies the link from the dialog that opens after creating the group, or later from
the group's Chat-Info (also as a QR code). It looks like this:

```text
https://<host>/login?gcid=<group id>&aid=<Beratungsstelle id>
```

- `gcid` names the group.
- `aid` names the group's Beratungsstelle. Before someone logs in, the app is not allowed to read
  the group. Without `aid` a newcomer would have to find the Beratungsstelle through the normal
  topic and postcode search, where a group's Beratungsstelle is often not listed. With `aid` the
  registration already knows the Beratungsstelle and its topic.
- `<host>` is always the server the link was copied on. A link copied on a test server leads back
  to that test server.

<Warning>
**Links shared before the `aid` change must be shared again.** Older links carry only `gcid`. A
newcomer who opens one ends on "Keine Online-Beratungsstelle gefunden". These old links will not
be repaired. Copy the link again from the group's Chat-Info and share the new one.
</Warning>

## 4.10.2 Who opens the link, and where they land

| Who opens the link | What they see | Where they end up |
|---|---|---|
| **Newcomer, no account** | The entry screen of the group: no steps, no topic or postcode questions. It opens on **"Ohne Konto beitreten"** (screen 0a). **"Konto anlegen"** switches to screen 0b. | The group's waiting room, then the group once a moderator starts it |
| **Client with an account** | The normal login | The group's waiting room |
| **Counsellor of the group's Beratungsstelle** | Their own group view with **"Chat starten"**, not the client's waiting room | The group, as moderator |
| **Counsellor of another Beratungsstelle** | A notice that they are not part of this group | Nowhere — see *knock to join* below |

**Knock to join (in progress).** A counsellor who is not a member will be able to ask to join. The
group's active moderators then see who is asking and can let them in. This is being built and is
not available yet.

## 4.10.3 Join without an account, or create an account

The entry screen offers two ways in. Both ask for the data-protection consent, because group
messages and calls are transmitted and encrypted.

| | **Ohne Konto beitreten** (0a) | **Konto anlegen** (0b) |
|---|---|---|
| What the person enters | a name (a pseudonym is suggested) | a name and a password they choose |
| Password | created for them and never shown | chosen and shown |
| Coming back later | not possible once the browser session ends | log in again at any time; the group is in their conversation list |
| Button | "Der Gruppe beitreten" | "Registrieren" |

A person who joins without an account still gets a real account on the server. They simply never
learn its password. Deleting such accounts automatically after the group is backend work that
does not exist yet (ORISO-UserService#1001). Until it does, these accounts stay.

**While the registration runs**, the screen shows the group's own three cards (how the group
takes place, alias only, new dates and links) and "Zur Gruppe". It never promises an answer from
a counselling centre, because a group join opens no enquiry (see 4.10.4).

## 4.10.4 Joining a group opens no counselling enquiry

**The rule:** registering through a group's invite link makes the person a member of **that
group** and nothing else. It does **not** open a counselling enquiry (*Beratungsanfrage*) at the
group's Beratungsstelle.

**Why.** A person who joins a Gesprächskreis has asked for the group, not for one-to-one
counselling. Until this change, the link used the ordinary registration, and the ordinary
registration always opens a counselling conversation at the chosen Beratungsstelle. So every
group join also left an empty counselling conversation behind:

- the client saw a counselling conversation in their list that they never asked for;
- as soon as the client had given their privacy consent, that conversation could show up in the
  counsellors' list of open enquiries, where nobody expected it and nobody was meant to answer it.

On the development server, test joins on 22–23.09.2026 left exactly such conversations behind
(sessions 211 and 213).

**What happens now.** The registration names the group. The server checks that the group is a
self-help group of that Beratungsstelle, creates the account, and adds the person to the group.
No counselling conversation is created. A client who later wants one-to-one counselling asks for
it separately, like any other client.

A group-only client has an account with one group in it and no counselling conversation. Their
account data loads normally, and the group is what they see after logging in.

## 4.10.5 Privacy statement, Datenschutz and Impressum

**Before the first message: the Beratungsstelle's privacy statement.** Decided by Frank on
23.09.2026. A client in a group whose agreement is not on record sees the statement of the
Beratungsstelle that runs the group before they can write. This is the second consent gate of
[ADR-022](/decisions/adr-022), the same dialog as in the live chat. It replaces the conversation
until the client agrees. The composer stays hidden until then.

- **Who is asked.** Every group client whose agreement is not on record. ADR-022 decides this gate
  by consent state and forbids guessing anonymity, so the app never asks whether an account is
  temporary. A registration through the invite link records no agreement. That means both the
  temporary join (0a) and the full account created through the link (0b) are asked once. A client
  who already had an account, for example from counselling, has an agreement on record and is not
  asked again. Counsellors are never asked.
- **Which text.** The consent sentence of the group's Beratungsstelle for its topic, when it has
  one. A Beratungsstelle without its own statement shows the fixed warning that no statement is
  available. When the group's topic cannot be told (the Beratungsstelle has several), the
  platform's sentence applies.
- **Where it is recorded.** On the account, as the live chat records it. A group has no counselling
  conversation to hold a per-room pointer. The known limit: someone who agreed for one
  Beratungsstelle is not asked again in a group of another one. Closing that gap needs a pointer
  per group membership (backend).

**Datenschutz and Impressum, like in every chat room.** Everyone in a running group — client or
counsellor, text or audio/video — finds both in the ⋮ menu of the room. In the client's waiting
room they sit under the stage on desktop and in a ⋮ menu "Rechtliches" in the top bar on a phone.
Both open the documents of the Beratungsstelle that runs the group.

## 4.10.6 For developers

- `POST /users/askers/new` accepts an optional `groupChatId`. When it is set, the registration
  assigns that group (`user_chat`) and creates **no** session. The response is `201` as before.
- The server answers `400` and creates **no** account when the group does not exist, is not a
  self-help group (`conversation_type = SELF_HELP`), does not belong to the `agencyId` in the same
  request, or when `consultantId` is also set (a consultant link opens a one-to-one enquiry).
- The frontend sends `groupChatId` only when the link carries `aid` and the person registers at
  exactly that Beratungsstelle. A registration at an agency picked in the steps stays an ordinary
  counselling registration.
- If a later step of the registration fails, the group membership is removed again together with
  the account (provisioning compensation `GROUP_CHAT_MEMBERSHIP`).
- After registration the app follows the link as before: `PUT /users/chat/<id>/assign` answers
  `409` because the person is already a member, and the app opens the waiting room.
- Deploy order: the backend ignores unknown fields and the old frontend never sends
  `groupChatId`, so either side can go first. The rule takes effect once both are deployed.
